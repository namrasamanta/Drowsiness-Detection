😴 Drowsiness Detection runs live on your camera

Before you start

  • Good, even light on your face — sit facing a light source or window. Avoid a bright window/lamp behind you (backlight makes your face too dark to detect).
  • Face the camera directly, roughly at eye level, about an arm's length away — your whole face inside the frame.
  • No sunglasses — your eyes must be visible. Regular glasses are fine if there's no strong glare on the lenses.
  • One person in view — with multiple faces, only the largest (closest) one is tracked.
  • Hold still the first few seconds — look at the camera normally while it calibrates to your eyes and posture.
  • Keep your volume on — alerts beep in the browser.

How it works

Sit facing the camera. Small head turns are fine, but beyond about 25° to either side your eyes can't be measured, so checks pause — and staying turned away for a few seconds raises a distraction alert.

What it detects

  • 😴 Microsleep — eyes closed for 3 s straight → alarm
  • 👁️ Fatigue (PERCLOS) — eyes closed more than 30% of the last 30 s → warning
  • 🥱 Yawning — 2 or more yawns within a minute → warning
  • 🔴 Head drop — head pitched down ~20° for 1.5 s (nodding off) → alarm
  • 👀 Looking away — turned more than 25° sideways: 3 s → warning, 6 s → alarm
  • 🚫 Face not visible — head fully down, covered, or seat empty: 2 s → warning, 4 s → alarm
  • 🎯 Auto-calibration — the first few seconds learn your normal eye openness and posture, so thresholds adapt to you and to the camera angle.
